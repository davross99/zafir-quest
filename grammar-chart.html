Grammar Chart

Prepositions of Position

Nine words for where things are.

Higher, Lower, Across Above · Below · Over
Above

Higher than something else, with a space between them.

The clock hangs above the fireplace.

Below

Lower than something else, with a space between them.

The cat is asleep below the kitchen table.

Over

Across from one side to the other, high above — or covering something with contact.

She threw a blanket over the sofa.

How Close Near · Close To · Next To
Near

Means the same as "close to." Never use the word "to" with "near."

The bus stop is near the supermarket.

Close To

Means the same as "near." Always use the word "to" with "close."

The bus stop is close to the supermarket.

Next To

Direct contact or immediate adjacency — side by side, touching.

She parked her bike next to the front door.

Hidden Or Facing Behind · In Front Of · Between
Behind

On the other side of something — hidden by it, out of view.

The dog hid behind the sofa when it heard thunder.

In Front Of

Directly facing something, or standing between you and it — clearly visible.

He parked the car in front of the house.

Between

In the space that separates two things.

Jack sat between his mother and his father — his mother on the left, his father on the right.

AT, IN, and ON appear in the story but are not gap-fill targets in this game.
